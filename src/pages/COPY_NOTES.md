The reader is an owner who already sets goals for the quarter. Talk to that person the way Joshua would across a table. Contractions. Short sentences and longer ones in the same section. Plain words an owner uses. Second person: “you” and “your.” AIify speaks as “we.” No prospect first person.

Read a sentence out loud. If a person would not say it, rewrite it. No slogan fragments and no tidy three-beat taglines.

## Words that do not belong on this page

Don’t use these as abstract nouns: follow-through, systems, leverage, lever, aim, mechanism, outcomes, transform. Name the thing instead. A CRM. A follow-up after a quote. A price in a doc. A task with a name on it. A draft waiting for an OK.

“The Lever Loop” came off the eyebrow for that reason. The eyebrow is “AIify.” The H1 stays exactly: “Get your quarterly goals done without hiring anyone or learning AI.”

The old subhead (“You set the goals. We bring the people, the systems and the follow-through.”) was a three-beat tagline and used two banned words. It is gone. The line under the H1 now says who shows up and that you say OK before anything goes out.

“We do the digging. We do the building.” was a slogan. It is gone. The spoken version is that you keep running the company, and we do the setup, the checking, and the fixing.

## The line about time

Kept, with contractions, because it sounds like speech: “You're not short on ideas or ambition. You're short on time.”

## What the page has to explain

DIY fails because the tools are cheap and look easy. Somebody still has to figure out where AI fits, set it up, connect it to the tools you already use, check the work, and fix it when it breaks. You don’t have that person. Hiring one is slow and expensive, and you can’t tell a good one from a bad one until the quarter is gone.

What you get is a team: a chief of staff who runs the account, developers, and a project manager assigned to you. They use AI tools every day on your goals. AI is the tool. Our people run it. No personal names. No headcount. The older extra job titles are not on the page.

Five things, each with what it does, a concrete picture, and why it matters:

1. The CRM updates itself from call and meeting recordings. Salespeople don’t type the notes. The next step doesn’t stay in someone’s head.
2. Automations we build, test, and keep running. Examples: follow-ups, quotes, reminders.
3. A company knowledge base: docs, prices, policies, and past answers in one place the team and the AI can ask.
4. A meeting watcher. It listens and turns “we should” into an assigned task and a draft.
5. An AI agent set up for the company. It plans and does the tool-work toward the goals. Our team checks it. Do not name a vendor. Do not say “RAG.”

How it starts, in order: sit in the quarterly goal-setting, pick the goal that matters most, build the piece that moves that goal beside the work the team already does, then go to the next one.

Control: the AI drafts and lines work up, a person reviews it, and nothing enters the software you run the company on or goes to a customer without your OK.

Weekly report: every department head gets a plain report of what got done for them and what moved, so people feel backed up, not replaced.
